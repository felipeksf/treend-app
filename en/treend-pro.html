What Treend is and how it reads currency timing

Treend tells you whether a currency or an asset is cheap or expensive compared with its own history. It covers the dollar, euro, pound and yuan, plus Brazilian stocks, US stocks, real estate funds and commodities, and alerts you when a price enters an unusual range. Treend Pro costs US$ 199 per month, with an unconditional 7-day guarantee.

The difference is simple: almost every service in this market answers what is worth buying. Treend answers when.

  • A reading, not a chart. Each asset gets a number from 0 to 100 per timeframe, saying whether today's price is unusual for it.
  • An alert instead of a watch. You pick the currency or asset and get told when it enters the range you care about.
  • It is for spending, not only investing. It applies to converting a payment or paying a supplier as much as to buying a stock.

What exactly does Treend do?

Treend compares the current price of an asset with that asset's own history and returns a number from 0 to 100. A high number means the price is unusually low for that asset; a low number means the opposite. The reading exists across several timeframes, because someone converting money this week and someone investing for the year are not looking at the same thing.

Why does this matter if I only need to move money?

Because the day you convert often costs more than the fee you negotiated. Conversion spreads run from 1% to 5% of the amount. Exchange rates move far more than that within a single year. Negotiating the spread while converting on an arbitrary day means optimising the smaller of the two costs.

Who uses Treend?

Freelancers and professionals paid in a foreign currency, people paying suppliers abroad, travellers, and investors in stocks, real estate funds or commodities. What they share is not market expertise: it is having a date to choose.

What Treend is not

It is not a forecast. Treend does not say what the dollar will do tomorrow, does not tell you to buy or sell anything and does not promise an outcome. It says where today's price sits relative to that asset's past. The decision stays yours.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Switching provider and still converting on any random day. It cuts the spread and leaves the larger cost untouched.
  • Reading today's rate alone. A number means nothing without the history to compare it against.
  • Using a single timeframe. The week's reading is not the year's reading.
  • Waiting for the exact bottom. An unusual range lasts days or weeks; the low point is only visible afterwards.

How does Treend Pro solve it?

Treend Pro gives the 0-to-100 reading for currencies and for four markets, across several timeframes, and alerts you when the asset you chose enters the range you set. You configure it once and start choosing the date with a number in front of you instead of a hunch. It is US$ 199 per month, with an unconditional 7-day guarantee.
