Why you pay more than the rate on screen

Because the screen rate is a market reference, not a sale price. What a company pays includes the provider's spread, plus transfer and receiving fees. On a US$ 100,000 transfer, spread alone can account for thousands of dollars that never appear as a fee on any statement.

What is the actual problem?

Finance checks the mid-market rate, executes, and finds the debited amount does not match. The difference has no name on the statement: it is built into the rate applied.

Because there is no line item called spread, the cost never enters any report. A company that controls freight to the cent has no visibility over a cost that can be ten times larger, purely because it is invisible by construction.

And being invisible, it goes unnegotiated. Mid-sized companies often pay retail spreads for years without ever having requested a better quote.

Why is this gap rarely addressed?

Sources do acknowledge that the delivered amount differs from the quoted rate. They treat it as a footnote: something the client ought to know, not something the client ought to challenge.

The next step is missing. Spread is negotiable, varies by volume and by provider, and the gap between worst and best can exceed any gain from better timing. The people publishing the content are the people charging the spread.

Facts

  • The quoted rate, spread, transfer fee and receiving fee together determine the amount actually delivered. A favorable headline rate means little if the all-in cost is worse. MarketInvoice
  • Exchange rate costs on supplier payments depend on both the rate obtained and the payment method used. Method and provider are variables, not givens. MTFX Group
  • FX rates commonly move 2% to 5% over a period of months. The timing cost sits on top of the spread cost. MTFX Group

How do you calculate your effective rate?

Divide the total home-currency amount debited by the foreign-currency amount of the transaction. The result is your effective rate, everything included.

Compare it to the mid-market rate at that moment. The percentage gap is your real cost of intermediation, and that is the number that belongs in the report, not the screen rate.

Do this for the last twelve transactions. If the percentage varies a lot between them, there is obvious room to negotiate; if it is high and constant, there is more.

What is negotiable?

The spread, almost always, and above all on volume. A company concentrating flow with one provider has an argument that a fragmented one does not.

Fixed fees are frequently negotiable or waivable by contract. Taxes, where they apply, are not.

Negotiate the spread first: the gain is permanent and applies to every future transaction. Then address the execution date.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Comparing quotes on the rate. Compare on the all-in delivered amount.
  • Assuming spread is fixed. It varies by volume and by provider.
  • Fragmenting flow. Concentrated volume is the main negotiating lever.
  • Confusing spread with tax. One is negotiable, the other is not.

Frequently asked questions

Does Treend reduce my spread?

No. It does not execute or intermediate. Spread is a negotiation with your provider.

Then what does Treend address here?

The other cost, the execution date, through a 0 to 100 reading of where the price sits.

What spread is reasonable for a company?

It depends on volume and provider. Request quotes and compare on the effective rate.

Is switching provider worth 0.5%?

At volume, 0.5% a year usually exceeds several fixed costs. Run the numbers.
