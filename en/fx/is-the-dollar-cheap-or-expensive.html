How to know if the dollar is cheap or expensive

The dollar is only cheap or expensive relative to its own history, never from the rate alone. To tell, compare today's value with the range the currency has traded in over recent months and years, over the period that matches your decision, and add the spread your provider charges. Treend makes that comparison and returns a number from 0 to 100.

Facts

  • Banks and providers add 1% to 5% in exchange-rate spread. The margin sits inside the rate you are quoted, not as a separate fee. Keytom
  • PayPal applies a conversion spread of 3% to 4%. The same conversion through a dedicated FX provider costs materially less. CanAm Currency
  • Dedicated providers price liquid pairs under 1%. That is the floor you can compare any quote against. Seev Cash
  • Holding foreign currency briefly can improve the outcome. The standard advice for anyone not in urgent need of local currency is to watch the rate and convert on a better one. Grey

Why does today's rate not answer the question?

Because a number on its own has no reference. Knowing the rate tells you nothing about cheap or expensive: it depends on where the currency has been. The same rate can be the quarter's lowest and the year's highest. Without the comparison to history, you are reading a number, not an answer.

Which history should I compare against?

The period of your decision. If you are converting this week, what matters is where the rate sits against recent weeks. If you are paid every month and can wait, the range of recent years is what counts. This is why one reading over one period misleads: the same rate can be cheap for the year and expensive for the month.

How do I know what the conversion will really cost?

Add the provider's spread, any fixed fee and any local tax to the market rate. The result is the effective rate, and that is what you compare between providers. The headline rate on a screen is not comparable on its own.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Comparing one provider's headline rate with another's effective rate. They are different numbers.
  • Reading only the day's movement. A 1% drop does not tell you the level is low.
  • Using a five-year chart for a decision about tomorrow. The comparison window has to match the decision.
  • Negotiating the spread and converting on any day. The margin is 1% to 5%; the day can cost more.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a rate that counts as expensive?

No. The same rate is cheap or expensive depending on recent history and on the period your decision covers.

Will the dollar go up or down?

Nobody answers that. What can be measured is where today's price sits relative to the currency's past.

Should I look at the market rate or the effective rate?

The market rate to know what the currency is worth, the effective rate with spread and fees to know what you will pay.

Does Treend cover other currencies?

Yes: the euro, pound and yuan, as well as the dollar.
