Importers: buy at shipment or at due date?

Neither by default. Shipment and due date are logistics milestones, not price criteria. Between them sits a window, often 60 to 90 days, and what should set the purchase day is where the price stands within the range that currency has traded in.

What is the actual problem?

The importer has two obvious dates and picks one by habit. Buying at shipment feels safe: the landed cost is fixed and the margin on the order is known. Buying at the due date preserves cash for as long as possible.

Both are defensible, and neither looks at the price. They are decisions about risk and about cash, taken as if they were decisions about currency.

On a US$ 150,000 import, with rates commonly moving 2% to 5% across months, the gap between a good and a poor day inside the same window easily exceeds US$ 3,000. That is order margin, set by the shipping calendar.

Why is this gap rarely addressed?

Import guides explain contract types well and when each is available. The content is technically sound.

What they do not do is help choose the day among the available ones. The operational question "do I buy today or in three weeks?" never appears, because answering it would require a price reference the material does not have and the bank does not supply.

Facts

How do you define the real window?

It starts when cash or facility is available. It ends at the due date minus settlement days. Between them is your window, and it belongs in the process, not in someone's head.

One detail changes everything: if the order margin is thin and an 8% move erases the profit, part of the purchase should be hedged early regardless of price. Certainty and price are separate decisions and can apply to different parts of the same order.

How do you choose the day inside it?

By the position of the price. Treend returns a 0 to 100 reading of where today's rate sits within that currency's own historical range, across several timeframes.

It is not a forecast and there is no promised outcome. It lets you replace "we buy at the due date because that is the process" with "we buy when the price is in the lower part of the range, and at the deadline if that does not happen".

What are the common mistakes?

  • Choosing between two logistics milestones. Neither says anything about price.
  • Treating the whole order the same. Part may need certainty and part may not.
  • Ignoring settlement days. They shorten the real window.
  • Buying on the bank's call. Their calendar is not your window.

Frequently asked questions

What if the supplier demands payment up front?

Then the window is short or absent. Put the effort into the spread.

Does Treend book the contract?

No. Execution stays with your bank or provider.

Does it work for imports priced in yuan?

Yes, each currency read against its own history.

How does it apply to forward contracts?

Choosing the day to enter a forward is the same decision: the rate you contract reflects where the currency sits today.
