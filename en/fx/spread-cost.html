How much the FX spread costs and how to pay less

The FX spread costs between 1% and 5% of every conversion and is built into the rate, never shown as a fee. A 2% spread on a US$ 5,000 monthly payment costs US$ 1,200 a year. To pay less, compare the effective rate rather than the headline rate, and move off platform defaults like PayPal.

Facts

  • Banks and providers add 1% to 5% in spread. It is a margin applied to the market rate, invisible as a line item. Keytom
  • A 2% spread on US$ 5,000 a month costs US$ 1,200 a year. On US$ 50,000 of annual income, 1% costs US$ 500 and 3% costs US$ 1,500. Keytom
  • PayPal applies 3% to 4% on conversion. For business accounts the spread sits on top of the transaction rate. CanAm Currency
  • Dedicated providers price liquid pairs under 1%. That is the realistic floor for a common pair. Seev Cash

Why does the spread not appear on the invoice?

Because it is not charged as a fee: it is applied as a margin on the rate. The provider takes the market rate, adds its margin and shows you the result as if it were the price of the currency. That is how two services can both advertise "no fees" and deliver different amounts for the same dollar.

How do I calculate the spread I am paying?

Divide the rate you were quoted by the market reference rate, subtract 1 and multiply by 100. Add any fixed fee and local tax to get the effective cost. That effective number is the only one worth comparing between providers.

How do I actually pay less?

Compare effective rates before converting, avoid converting inside the payment platform by default, and consolidate volume where pricing improves with size. But there is a second cost, larger than the spread and almost always ignored: the day you convert. Going from a 4% spread to 1% saves 3%; major currency pairs move several times that within a year.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Comparing headline rates across providers. The valid comparison is of effective rates.
  • Believing "no fees". The margin can be entirely inside the rate.
  • Letting the payment platform convert by default. That is usually the most expensive route.
  • Driving the spread to zero and converting on any day. It saves the smaller cost and ignores the larger one.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as an acceptable spread?

Under 1% is achievable on liquid pairs; 1% to 5% is the common range, and platform defaults sit at the top of it.

Is the spread negotiable?

On larger amounts, yes. Rates are not fixed, and pricing improves with volume.

Does Treend get me a better rate?

No. It does not execute conversions; the rate stays your provider's.

Is cutting the spread enough to save money?

It helps, but the day of the conversion usually weighs more, because currency pairs move more in a year than providers differ.
