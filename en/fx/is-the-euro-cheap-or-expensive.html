How to know if the euro is cheap or expensive

The euro is cheap or expensive relative to its own history, never from the rate alone. Compare today's value with the range the currency has traded in over recent months and years, over the period matching your decision, and add the spread your provider charges. Treend makes that comparison and returns a number from 0 to 100.

Facts

  • Banks and providers add 1% to 5% in spread. This applies to the euro as to the dollar: the margin is inside the quoted rate. Keytom
  • Dedicated providers price liquid pairs under 1%. The euro is a liquid pair, so that floor is reachable. Seev Cash
  • PayPal applies 3% to 4% on conversion. Converting euros inside the payment platform is the most expensive default. CanAm Currency
  • Holding foreign currency briefly can improve the outcome. The advice holds for anyone without an urgent need for local currency. Grey

Why does today's euro rate not answer the question?

Because a number without a reference has no meaning. The same euro rate can be the quarter's lowest and the highest of the past two years. Saying whether it is expensive requires comparing it with the currency's own history, and only that turns a rate into an answer.

Which period should the comparison use?

The period of your decision. Converting this week means comparing against recent weeks. Being paid in euros monthly and able to hold means comparing against recent years. A single reading misleads: the same euro can be cheap for the year and expensive for the month.

Does the euro behave like the dollar?

Not necessarily. The euro has its own price range and its own periods of strength and weakness, and can move in a different direction from the dollar in the same month. That is why the comparison has to use the euro's history, rather than being carried over from a dollar reading.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Using a dollar reading to decide about the euro. They are currencies with different histories.
  • Comparing headline rates across providers. The valid comparison is of effective rates.
  • Reading only the day's movement. A 1% drop does not tell you the level is low.
  • Using a long period for a decision about tomorrow. The comparison window has to match the decision.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a rate that counts as an expensive euro?

No. The same rate is cheap or expensive depending on recent history and the period of your decision.

Will the euro go up or down?

Nobody answers that. What is measured is where today's price sits relative to the currency's past.

Does Treend cover the euro as well as the dollar?

Yes: the euro, pound and yuan, with the same 0-to-100 reading.

Does it work for someone paid in euros, not only buying them?

Yes. A recipient wants a high rate and a buyer wants a low one; the reading is the same, only the direction of the decision changes.
