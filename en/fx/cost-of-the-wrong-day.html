What executing FX on the wrong day costs

It depends on volume and on how far the currency moved. On a US$ 100,000 transaction, a 2% gap between the day you chose and a better day in the same week is US$ 2,000. Across a year of transactions the total usually exceeds the cost of any tool or adviser.

What is the actual problem?

Companies measure FX cost by spread and fees, because those appear on the statement. The cost of the execution date appears nowhere: there is no ledger line called "we dealt on a bad Thursday".

Being invisible, it goes unmanaged. A finance team will negotiate 0.3% off the spread over several weeks and give up 3% by picking the date badly in the same month, without anyone noticing.

Why does it never show up?

Because there is no benchmark. Your records show the rate you dealt at; they never show the range of rates available across your decision window.

Without that reference there is no way to say whether the trade was good or poor, and what cannot be judged cannot be improved. It is why companies with organized treasury still cannot answer whether they execute well.

Facts

  • FX rates commonly move 2% to 5% over a period of months. That is the amplitude the execution date works within. MTFX Group
  • Every extra day between contract and settlement enlarges the period in which an adverse move can occur. MarketInvoice
  • A defensible FX process rests on documented triggers rather than directional feelings. Alt21

How do you size it for your business?

Take three numbers you already have: annual volume converted, the range the currency moved across the period, and how many transactions you ran.

Using a conservative slice of that range, say 2% between nearby days, a company moving US$ 1 million a year has US$ 20,000 in play. That is not guaranteed gain: it is the size of what is exposed to the choice of date, and therefore the ceiling of what better process can address.

Run it with your real volume before deciding the topic deserves attention. If the number is small, you have just saved yourself the effort.

What can realistically be recovered?

Not the best day. Nobody knows which it was until afterwards, and Treend does not forecast. What can change is the habit of filling in the poor part of the range because no rule exists.

Moving from "always in the worst third" to "on average in the middle" is material at volume, and it is reachable with a documented rule rather than luck. That is the honest claim.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Measuring only spread. It is the visible cost, not the largest.
  • Benchmarking against the next day. The reference is the range across the window.
  • Looking at trades one at a time. The cost shows in the annual sum.
  • Blaming the market. Volatility is given; the date is your choice.

Frequently asked questions

Can the best day be known in advance?

No. No tool knows, and you should distrust any that claims to. What is knowable is where today's price sits in that currency's history.

Does this matter at low volume?

Low volume means little at stake. Run the arithmetic before investing attention.

Does Treend guarantee recovering the difference?

No. There is no promised return. It supplies the reference; the result depends on your window and your discipline.

Can we assess trades already done?

Yes, by comparing the rate dealt against the range of that period.
