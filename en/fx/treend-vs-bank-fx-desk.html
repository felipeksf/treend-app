Treend or your bank's FX desk advice?

The desk quotes, executes and settles, and earns from the spread on that trade. Treend executes nothing and earns nothing from your decision: it charges US$ 199 per month and returns a 0 to 100 reading on where the price sits. They coexist, with different incentives.

What is the actual problem?

The FX desk calls, comments on the market and suggests this looks like a good level. The service is genuinely good and the information feels advisory. But the person on the other side is paid from the spread, and only gets paid if you deal.

That does not make them dishonest. It makes them someone whose incentive is that the transaction happens, not that it happens on the best day for you. Sometimes those align and sometimes they do not.

The result is that companies make the highest-impact financial decision of the month on the view of someone compensated by the volume of that decision.

Why is this gap rarely addressed?

Because no neutral content exists on it. Desks, brokers and payment providers all position themselves as advisory, and none will publish a page explaining its own conflict of interest.

So the client never sees the question framed: whoever gives me this information, what do they earn when I act on it? It is a checkable question that requires trusting no one — you only have to look at where the revenue comes from.

Facts

  • Waiting to hedge while hoping the rate moves your way is speculation, not strategy. A documented trigger is what a treasurer can defend to the board. Alt21
  • The quoted rate, spread and fees together determine the delivered amount. Spread is how execution providers are paid. MarketInvoice
  • FX rates commonly move 2% to 5% over a period of months. That is the range a "deal now" suggestion sits within. MTFX Group

What does each one do better?

The desk does what Treend does not: quotes, executes, settles, resolves documentation and negotiates spread with you. That is indispensable and no tool replaces it.

Treend does what the desk has no incentive to do: indicate that today may not be a good level. The 0 to 100 reading compares the current price with that currency's own history. It does not forecast, does not promise outcomes, and does not change its view with anyone's quarterly target.

Used together, you arrive at the desk already knowing where the price sits, and the conversation shifts from "what do you think?" to "what spread can you do?".

How do you use both without friction?

Keep the relationship and use the desk for what it is good at: execution and spread. Bring the date decision already formed.

In practice: define the window internally, follow the timing reading, and call the desk when the trigger fires. The relationship manager still books the trade; what changes is who decides the day.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Mistaking good service for impartiality. Service quality does not change where revenue comes from.
  • Negotiating the day but never the spread. With a desk, spread is what is actually contestable.
  • Dealing on the call. The call follows their calendar, not your window.
  • Switching provider expecting neutrality. The conflict belongs to the model, not the institution.

Frequently asked questions

Does Treend execute FX?

No. It does not quote, deal or settle. Execution stays with your provider.

Does Treend receive commission from banks?

No. Revenue is the US$ 199 monthly subscription, and nothing changes whether you deal or not.

Do we have to stop using the desk?

No. Use both, each for what it does best.

Is it useful if we already get tight spreads?

Yes. Spread and timing are separate costs; improving one does not address the other.
