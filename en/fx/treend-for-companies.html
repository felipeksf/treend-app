How a company uses Treend day to day

One person in finance adds the currencies the company trades, defines the range considered favorable in each, and receives an alert when it is reached. The decision stays internal and execution stays with the bank. Treend enters as a price reference, not as an execution venue.

What is the actual problem?

The company already has a bank, a relationship manager and a payment process. What it does not have is its own reference on the price of the currency: something that states, with no interest in the trade happening, whether today is high or low for that currency.

Without it, the decision rests on two poor inputs: the screen rate, which says nothing about level, and the view of someone paid from the spread.

That specific gap is what Treend fills. It does not replace the bank, the treasury function or the internal policy.

Why is this gap rarely addressed?

Existing tools fall into two groups. Execution platforms, offered by whoever intermediates the trade. And treasury systems built for accounting: consolidating rates, closing the books, feeding the ERP.

Neither answers "is today's price good?". The first has no incentive; the second does not have the question in scope. Mid-sized companies end up with neither.

Facts

  • FX rates commonly move 2% to 5% over a period of months. Each currency has its own range, and today's price is read against it. MTFX Group
  • A defensible FX process rests on documented triggers rather than directional feelings. An alert on a defined range is such a trigger. Alt21
  • The quoted rate, spread and fees determine the delivered amount. Treend does not change any of those costs: execution stays with your provider. MarketInvoice

What does the finance routine look like?

Setup takes one session: add the currencies the company actually trades and define, for each, the 0 to 100 range considered favorable for execution.

After that there is no monitoring routine. The alert arrives when the range is reached, and the responsible person checks whether a window is open and cash is available. If so, they execute with the bank; if not, they wait for the next one.

Time spent goes down rather than up, because nobody checks the rate daily any more.

Who in the company should have access?

Whoever decides and whoever reviews. Typically the analyst who operates and the manager who audits the quarterly result.

Giving access to many people tends to make things worse: the reading becomes a topic of opinion in meetings when it should be a written rule applied by one person. The value is in consistency.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Adding currencies the company does not trade. Alerts lose meaning when they fire constantly.
  • Expecting Treend to execute. It informs; the bank executes.
  • Using it with no defined window. Without a deadline there is nothing for the alert to trigger.
  • Changing the range every week. Without stability there is nothing to evaluate.

Frequently asked questions

Does Treend book FX trades?

No. It does not quote, execute or intermediate.

Is this investment advice?

No. There is no recommendation, no rate forecast and no promised outcome.
