Who decides FX timing in your company?

In most mid-sized companies it is whoever is available: the owner, a finance analyst, sometimes the freight forwarder. The month's highest-impact financial decision gets made on a screen rate and the bank's view, with no written criterion and no review afterwards.

What is the actual problem?

Large companies have a treasury function, with dedicated people and systems. Mid-sized importers and exporters, which is most of them, have no such role.

So the decision drifts to whoever is left. Finance executes because a payment is due. The owner executes because of something read in the news. Nobody has the information needed, and nobody is held to the outcome, because the outcome is never measured.

It creates an odd vacuum: the company has an owner for purchasing, for freight and for tax, and none for the variable that moves the final cost most.

Why is this gap rarely addressed?

Because all available treasury material assumes a structure these companies do not have. It speaks of policy committees, mandates, integrated systems.

For a three-person finance team that content is unusable, so it gets ignored. The literature serves the company that already solved the problem and leaves out precisely those losing the most money to it.

Facts

  • A hedging process starts from your exposure and risk tolerance, then builds a repeatable process around it. Naming a decision owner is the first step. XE
  • Waiting while hoping the rate improves is speculation, not strategy. A named owner with a written trigger is what separates them. Alt21
  • FX rates commonly move 2% to 5% over a period of months. That is the amplitude an ownerless decision sits on. MTFX Group

How do you name an owner without creating a role?

Nobody needs to be hired. Three lines need to be written: who decides, within what window, on what trigger.

It can be the same analyst who already operates. The difference is that they now decide against a rule rather than an impression, and report on the quarterly result, which is now measurable.

That small formalization is usually worth more than any tool, because it turns an orphan decision into an owned one.

What information does that person need?

Two things. The window: from when they may execute to when they must. And the position of the price: within what this currency usually trades at, is today high, middle or low?

The second exists in no internal system today. Treend returns it as a 0 to 100 reading, comparing each currency against its own history. It does not forecast and does not promise outcomes.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Leaving the decision to whoever is free. Availability is not a criterion.
  • Deciding on the day's news. By the time it reaches you it is priced in.
  • Never measuring afterwards. Without review nobody knows if they decide well.
  • Waiting until you have a treasury function. Three written lines already change the result.

Frequently asked questions

Does it have to be someone in finance?

Not necessarily, but it must be one person, with the window and the trigger in writing.

Does Treend decide for us?

No. It supplies the 0 to 100 reading. The decision and the accountability stay internal.

How many people should have access?

Whoever decides and whoever audits. The trigger must be checkable by a second person.

Does this work with one transaction a month?

Yes, and it is simpler: one monthly decision against a written rule.
