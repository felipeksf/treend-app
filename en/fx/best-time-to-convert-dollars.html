When is the best time to convert my dollars?

The best time to convert is when the rate is high relative to the currency's recent history, because you get more local currency for the same dollar. Since nobody predicts rates, the practical decision is to compare today's value with the range of recent months and convert while it sits at the top of that range.

Facts

  • A 2% spread on a US$ 5,000 monthly payment costs US$ 1,200 a year. On US$ 50,000 of annual income, 1% costs US$ 500 and 3% costs US$ 1,500. Keytom
  • Holding foreign currency briefly can improve the outcome. The standard advice for anyone not in urgent need of local currency is to watch the rate and convert on a better one. Grey
  • PayPal applies a conversion spread of 3% to 4%. Converting through the payment platform by default is the most expensive route. CanAm Currency
  • Dedicated providers price liquid pairs under 1%. Once you are near that floor, the remaining cost is the day you convert. Seev Cash

Why does the day matter more than the fee?

Because the gap between a good day and a bad day is wider than the gap between a good provider and a bad one. Spreads run from under 1% to 5%. Major currency pairs move by far more than that within a single year. Comparing five providers and converting on the first business day optimises the smaller cost.

How do I recognise that the rate is high?

By comparing it with the currency's own recent range, not with a number you remember. The useful question is not "is this rate high?" but "is this rate high for where the currency has been these past six months?". That needs the history for the period that matches your decision: weeks if you need the money now, months or years if you can wait.

What if I need the money now?

Then timing is not a choice, and guessing is worse than converting. The timing gain only exists for the part of the money that can wait. The practical move is to separate what pays this month's bills, convert that without drama, and apply the timing reading only to the surplus.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Converting everything the day the payment lands. That is the most arbitrary day possible, chosen by the client's calendar.
  • Waiting for the exact top. The high range lasts days or weeks; the peak is only identifiable afterwards.
  • Comparing providers and ignoring the date. It saves on the smaller cost and loses on the larger one.
  • Converting because of a headline. By the time you read it, it is in the price.

Frequently asked questions

Can I know the best day in advance?

No. What you can know is whether today's rate is high or low compared with the currency's recent history.

Should I wait for the rate to rise further?

Nobody answers that. The available criterion is where today's price sits in the range of recent months.

Should I convert everything or in parts?

It depends on how much local currency you need in the short term. The surplus is the part where the date matters.

Is it better to negotiate the spread or to pick the day?

Spreads run from under 1% to 5%; currency pairs move much more than that in a year. The day weighs more.
