Convert dollars all at once or gradually?

Convert at once what you need to spend in local currency over the coming months, and gradually whatever is left over. The portion with a fixed date gains nothing from waiting. The surplus is the only part where the day of conversion changes the outcome, and that is where comparing today's rate with the currency's history pays.

Facts

  • A 2% spread on a US$ 5,000 monthly payment costs US$ 1,200 a year. Each conversion pays that margin, which limits how many times it is worth splitting. Keytom
  • Holding foreign currency briefly can improve the outcome. The advice applies specifically to people not in urgent need of local currency. Grey
  • Dedicated providers price liquid pairs under 1%. Larger, fewer conversions tend to land nearer that floor than many small ones. Seev Cash
  • PayPal applies 3% to 4% on conversion. Splitting a payment into many conversions inside a platform multiplies the most expensive spread. CanAm Currency

Why is splitting not automatically better?

Because every conversion pays a spread. Splitting into many parts lowers the risk of catching one bad day, but multiplies the margin paid and, at smaller amounts, worsens the rate, since pricing improves with size. Splitting makes sense as a way not to concentrate everything on an arbitrary day, not as a rule of the more parts the better.

How much should I convert at once?

Whatever has a date. Rent, school fees, taxes and the coming months' bills need local currency on a known date: that portion gets converted without trying to guess, because waiting for a better rate against a deadline is a bet. Converting it in one go also avoids paying the spread repeatedly on the same money.

What should I do with the surplus?

Keep it in the foreign currency and convert when the rate is high relative to the currency's recent range. This is where the day is worth something, because no deadline is forcing the decision. The criterion is not a forecast: it is where today's price sits inside the currency's own history.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Converting everything the day the payment lands. The most arbitrary day of all, set by the client's calendar.
  • Splitting into ten equal parts. It pays the spread ten times, at worse rates on smaller amounts.
  • Leaving the month's bills unconverted to wait for a better rate. That turns a dated expense into a bet.
  • Converting the surplus because of a headline. By the time you read it, it is in the price.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an ideal split between the two parts?

There is no general number: the split is set by your dated expenses in local currency over the coming months.

How many conversions are worth it for the surplus?

Few and larger, because each one pays a spread and small amounts tend to get worse pricing.

What if the rate moves against me after I convert?

It happens. The aim is not to catch the peak, it is to avoid converting on an arbitrary day.

Is it worth keeping everything in foreign currency indefinitely?

That is a decision about which currency to hold wealth in, different from the timing decision this page covers.
