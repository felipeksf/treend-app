What FX volume makes Treend pay for itself

Treend Pro costs US$ 199 per month. It pays for itself when the difference you capture on execution exceeds that. At 1% better execution, roughly US$ 20,000 of monthly flow covers it. At 0.5%, roughly US$ 40,000. Below that, the arithmetic does not work, and you should not subscribe.

What is the actual problem?

Almost no company measures what the chosen execution date costs. Spread and fees appear on the statement, so they get managed. The cost of the day does not appear anywhere, so it does not.

That makes it impossible to judge whether any timing tool is worth paying for: there is no baseline to compare it against. This page exists so you can run that arithmetic before deciding, and skip the subscription if it does not clear the bar.

Why does nobody publish this threshold?

Because showing the floor means telling part of your audience not to buy. Vendors of FX tools and platforms rarely state the volume below which their product stops making sense.

We state it: below roughly US$ 20,000 a month in flow, Treend Pro does not justify its cost. That is information worth having whether or not you subscribe.

Facts

  • FX rates commonly move 2% to 5% over a period of months. That range is what execution timing works within. MTFX Group
  • Waiting while hoping the rate improves is speculation, not strategy. What a tool can add is a documented trigger, not a forecast. Alt21
  • Hedging tells you how to lock a rate, not when the timing is right to do so. The timing decision sits outside every hedging instrument. XE

How do you run the arithmetic?

Take your monthly converted volume and multiply by a conservative execution difference. Do not use your best outcome: use what you believe you could capture consistently.

At US$ 20,000 a month, 1% is US$ 200, which is the subscription. At US$ 100,000, 1% is US$ 1,000, five times the cost. At US$ 500,000, the subscription is a rounding error.

The arithmetic inverts at small volumes. A company converting US$ 3,000 a month would need to capture nearly 7% just to break even, which is not a reasonable expectation.

What if your volume is below that?

Then Treend Pro is probably not for you today, and saying so is more useful than selling. A fixed US$ 199 only makes sense against flow that justifies it.

Revisit it when volume grows, or if you add up exposures you currently treat separately. Export receipts, supplier payments and foreign-currency expenses often sit in the same company and are decided by different people.

Why is there no promised return?

Because no one can promise one. Treend does not forecast rates and does not guarantee outcomes. It compares today's price against that currency's own history and returns a 0 to 100 reading, so the execution decision stops being a directional feeling.

What you capture depends on how much the currency moves, how much date flexibility you actually have, and whether the rule is followed consistently.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Using your best case in the arithmetic. Model a conservative difference instead.
  • Ignoring spread. A 1% better day does not offset a badly negotiated 4% spread.
  • Counting flow you do not control. Payments executed automatically at due date are outside the decision.
  • Subscribing with no date flexibility. With no window, there is nothing for a timing reading to act on.

Frequently asked questions

What minimum volume makes sense?

As a conservative rule, from about US$ 20,000 per month. Below that, the subscription tends to cost more than the difference captured.

Is pricing per transaction or a percentage?

Neither. It is US$ 199 per month regardless of volume, so the proportional cost falls as volume rises.

Can I test it first?

Yes. There is an unconditional 7-day guarantee, with no justification required.

Does Treend execute the trade?

No. It informs the timing; execution stays with your bank or broker, at their spread.
